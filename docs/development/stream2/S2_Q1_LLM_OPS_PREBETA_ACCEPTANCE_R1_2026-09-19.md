## Incident-open flow and durable relations

The test starts an enabled `NO_SESSION` schedule due at
`2026-09-19T08:00:00Z`. Two independent scheduler repositories race to
materialize it; PostgreSQL uniqueness leaves exactly one
`health_scheduled_runs` row. Scheduler A claims and starts it, its lease expires,
and scheduler B reclaims it. Scheduler A's stale finalization is rejected.

The deterministic BROKEN result is persisted in `health_runs` with all contour
results and a bounded `STATE_TRANSITION_TRACE` evidence reference. The result is
classified by the repository and linked to the scheduled run. Incident
processing creates one active `health_incidents` episode. Notification
derivation creates one `INCIDENT_OPENED` intent whose payload contains only
bounded operational metadata and `health-run:<runId>` as a safe reference.

`HealthNotificationRunner` claims the intent and calls only
`DeterministicNotificationTestSink`. The stable notification dedup key is the
provider idempotency key. Finalization persists `DELIVERED`, sink delivery ID,
and delivery timestamp. Health target admin, incident admin, notification
admin, and aggregate diagnostics all read the same durable source state.

## Repeated failures, escalation, and stale order

Three BROKEN observations for one scope reuse the same incident episode. The
single OPENED intent reaches `groupCount=3` and `suppressionReason=COOLDOWN`;
no additional OPENED intent or logical sink delivery is produced.

A separate scope observes DRIFT and then BROKEN. The incident remains one
episode and exactly one `INCIDENT_ESCALATED` intent is derived at CRITICAL
severity. A later-observed weaker state is not allowed to downgrade the current
state, and a stale weaker run is ignored by timestamp/run ordering.

## Recovery and post-recovery episode

For one scope, BROKEN → HEALTHY resolves the active automatic incident once and
creates one `INCIDENT_RECOVERED` intent. Reprocessing the recovery run is a
NOOP. `firstSeenRunId` remains unchanged, `resolvedByRunId` is the recovery
run, and diagnostics removes the target from the active incident count.

A later genuine BROKEN observation creates a new incident key/episode and a new
OPENED intent. The prior episode remains historical RESOLVED data.

## Maintenance and UNKNOWN

An active BROKEN episode entering operator maintenance becomes `MAINTENANCE`;
its history is retained. The undelivered product OPEN intent is marked
`SUPPRESSED` with `MAINTENANCE_ENTERED`, and a maintenance visibility event is
derived. Repeated maintenance does not create an alert storm. A subsequent
persistent BROKEN observation reopens policy on the same episode, resumes the
existing OPEN intent, and does not create a duplicate original OPEN identity;
the accepted cooldown reason may remain visible while the intent is pending.

The deterministic controlled-browser-unavailable fixture uses the accepted
pre-identity UNKNOWN contract: C13 is the sole uncertain contour and other
contours are not observed. The run is `UNKNOWN`, is not incident-worthy, does
not create a product-break notification, and is visible separately in
diagnostics. `standard` and `work` provider surfaces remain separate.
